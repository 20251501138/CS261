# Problem Statement — UPSC Civil Services Examination & Merit Management System

Every year, more than a million aspirants apply for the UPSC Civil Services
Examination, but as a candidate the process of applying for and tracking the
exam feels scattered and stressful. I have to visit the UPSC website to fill
a long application form, then separately generate a fee challan or pay
online, and hope that my payment gets linked to my application in time —
otherwise my form is treated as incomplete and I lose my only attempt for
the year. My educational and identity documents (Aadhaar, category
certificate, age proof) are verified manually by UPSC staff, and if there is
a mismatch I often find out only at the admit-card stage, sometimes too late
to correct it. I depend on notice-board PDFs and news portals to know
whether my admit card is out, whether my paper has been evaluated, and when
interviews will be scheduled, because there is no single place that shows me
the exact status of my own application.

On the other side, UPSC officials who administer the exam face an equally
difficult problem: thousands of paper answer scripts have to be anonymised
and distributed to examiners, evaluation progress has to be tracked batch by
batch, and a legally defensible merit list has to be produced by combining
marks with reservation-category rules — all while candidates repeatedly
request re-evaluation of specific scripts within a fixed window after
results are declared. Interview boards then need a clean, verified list of
shortlisted candidates with their documents already confirmed, so that the
personality test can be scheduled without last-minute document disputes.

What is needed is a single **UPSC Civil Services Examination & Merit
Management System** that lets a candidate register once, submit an
application, pay the fee online, and track every subsequent stage (document
verification, admit card, examination, evaluation, merit list, interview,
final result) from one account. The system should let UPSC officials verify
documents, release admit cards on a scheduled date, assign evaluation
batches to examiners, handle re-evaluation requests within the permitted
window, and generate a rule-compliant, category-wise merit list — with
automatic notifications going out to candidates at every stage, so that no
one has to depend on notice boards to know what is happening with their own
application.
